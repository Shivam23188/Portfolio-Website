FleetDesk: five tabs into one glance.

Redesigning the internal operations dashboard of an early-stage logistics startup, so dispatchers catch delayed deliveries before customers call about them.

Role
Product design intern
Team
1 PM, 3 engineers, me
Tools
Figma, FigJam, Notion
Platform
Web (desktop-first)

On this page

Overview Problem Research Design Design system Outcome Learnings
Overview

The short version

The ops team managed hundreds of daily deliveries through a dashboard built quickly by engineers in the company's first months. It worked — but finding a problem meant switching between five tabs and a spreadsheet. I led the redesign of the core live deliveries view and built the first version of the component library.

Problem

Scattered information

Status, driver and customer data lived in different tabs.

Solution

Exception-first view

Show what needs attention first; everything else on demand.

Result

Shipped to production

Used daily by the ops team after launch.

The problem

Delays were found by customers, not the team

Dispatchers only learned a delivery was late when a customer called. The data to catch it existed — it was just buried.

"How might we help dispatchers spot and act on at-risk deliveries before the customer notices?"
Research

Sitting next to the ops team

I shadowed dispatchers during two peak shifts, interviewed four of them, and ran a quick card sort to see how they grouped information.

  • Dispatchers kept a paper list of "problem orders" because the tool had no way to flag them.
  • 90% of the time they only cared about late, unassigned or failed deliveries.
  • Calling the driver was the most common action — and took four clicks to find the number.
Design

Exceptions first, details on demand

  • Attention queue: a pinned list at the top showing late, unassigned and failed deliveries, sorted by urgency.
  • Side panel instead of new tabs: clicking a delivery opens driver, customer and timeline in one panel — with "Call driver" one click away.
  • Status colour system: five states, colour plus icon and label, so it works for colour-blind users too.
  • Saved filters: each dispatcher can pin views for their own zone.
See alert→Open side panel→Call / reassign→Resolved
Design system

Building the foundations

There was no design system, so every screen looked slightly different. Using my engineering background, I set up tokens and components that mapped one-to-one to the team's React code.

Tokens

Colour, type, spacing

Named the same in Figma and in code, so handoff needed no translation.

Components

40+ documented

Tables, badges, panels and form fields — each with states and usage notes.

Learnings

What the internship taught me

  • Watching people work beats asking what they want — the paper list told me more than any interview.
  • In a startup, a good-enough system shipped this sprint beats a perfect one next quarter.
  • Speaking the engineers' language earned me a seat in technical discussions early on.
