Kharcha: budgeting in five seconds.

Designing a mobile expense tracker for college students who have tried budgeting apps — and quit all of them within a week.

Role
Solo product designer
Timeline
6 weeks
Tools
Figma, FigJam, Maze
Platform
Android & iOS

On this page

Overview Problem Research Design Testing Outcome Learnings
Overview

The short version

Most budgeting apps are built for salaried adults with bank-linked accounts. Students pay in cash and UPI, split bills constantly, and get pocket money in irregular chunks. Kharcha is designed around those habits: log an expense in one tap, split it in two, and see what's left for the week — not the month.

Problem

Logging is a chore

Users quit because adding an expense took 6+ taps.

Solution

One-tap logging

Smart presets for the places students spend most.

Result

Under 5 seconds

Average time to log dropped in the final prototype.

The problem

Money disappears in small amounts

Chai, auto rides, photocopies, late-night food orders. Individually tiny, together they're the reason students are broke by the 20th. Existing apps treat each of these as a form to fill — so people simply stop logging.

"How might we make tracking small, frequent expenses feel effortless enough that students keep doing it?"
Research

Listening before sketching

I interviewed 12 students across three colleges, ran a short survey, and reviewed four popular finance apps to see where people dropped off.

  • 8 of 12 had tried a budgeting app and abandoned it within two weeks.
  • Monthly budgets felt meaningless — pocket money and freelance income arrive irregularly.
  • Splitting with friends was the #1 source of "lost" money.
  • People remembered where they spent more easily than how much.

Who I designed for

Persona

Riya, 20 — hostel student

Gets money from home every few weeks. Spends mostly via UPI, splits food orders almost daily, wants to stop asking parents for "extra".

Key insight

Weeks, not months

Students think in "how much can I spend until Sunday", so the core number should be a weekly allowance.

Design

From six taps to one

I mapped the old logging flow and stripped out every step that didn't earn its place.

Open app→Tap a preset→Adjust amount (optional)→Done

Key decisions

  • Learned presets: the home screen shows your five most frequent spends ("Canteen ₹40", "Auto ₹60") — one tap logs them.
  • Weekly allowance ring: a single, glanceable number of what's left until Sunday.
  • Split in two taps: pick friends, Kharcha remembers who owes what.
  • No mandatory categories: the place is the category, which matched how people recall spending.
Testing

Three rounds, three fixes

I tested clickable prototypes with 5 students per round, measuring time-to-log and noting every hesitation.

Round 1

Icons confused people

Swapped abstract icons for text labels on presets.

Round 2

Undo was hidden

Added a 5-second undo toast after each one-tap log.

Round 3

Splits felt heavy

Recent friends now appear first, cutting a full step.

Outcome

What changed

0

average time to log an expense (from ~19s)

0

testers completed a split without help

0

interviewees said they'd use it daily

Learnings

What I'd do next

  • Speed beats features. Every screen I removed made the product better.
  • Test with real numbers — placeholder amounts hid layout issues with ₹10,000+ values.
  • Next: explore UPI SMS parsing to log payments automatically, with clear privacy controls.
